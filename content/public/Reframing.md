### Conceptual
Every concept in your mind has a known context, both are derived from your experience.
As a child, it is surprising to see your teacher at the supermarket as they're removed from their known context, this causes you to reframe your teacher as the wider concept of person. This is an insight.

Insightful 
> I wanted to buy a candle holder, but the store didn't have one. So I got a cake. - Mitch Hedberg

Reframing a cake as a candle holder. This conceptual joke could be in a different scheme, e.g. metaphor / simile
> A cake is an edible candle holder.

This first is funnier because the listener is first primed with the context of candle holders, 
The [Circle of expectations](public/Circle%20of%20expectations.md) includes: candelabra, but not "cake"
the context then shifts to cake as an example of a candle holder. 

Impact is greatest when the two concepts are cognitively distant and the association is congruent.
If the concepts are close, the observation is facile. If the association is far fetched the observation is unbelievable.

Multiple contexts? Connotations?

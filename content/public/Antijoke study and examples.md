"Meaning is use" - Wittgenstein

The sincere use of the word "anti-joke" defines its own meaning in context.
I.e. if it's called an anti-joke it is an anti-joke.

I have discovered several linguistic joke structures under the label of “Antijoke”
* Tautology
* non-sequitur (absurd, surreal, random, I don’t get it)
* Meta Joke (plays ok existing joke tropes)
* Shaggy dog story
* Traditional joke (misdirection, pun, forced reinterpretation, etc…)
Anti- prefix
Usually means “against” or “opposite of”, “opposition to”


Antichrist, Anticlockwise, Antibiotic, Antiviral, Antiseptics, Anti aircraft, Antisemitic, Antiwar, Antigravity, Antifreeze, Antisocial, Anticlimax

Antihero: 	lacking the qualities of a conventionally hero, but not villainous.
Antijoke:	lacking the qualities of a conventionally joke, but not unfunny.

Antiheroes aren’t defined by being unheroic, as antijokes aren’t defined as being unfunny

An antijoke is an obvious answer to a joke like question.
The punchline has no connection to the premise besides the obvious.
The punchline is so obvious you’re oblivious to predicting it.

There is the expectation of a traditional punchline, but instead are surprised by a totally, usually literally true, answer.

The punchline is either so straightforward that it isn't surprising like a joke
A punchline so outlandish and such a non sequitur that the shock makes it funny.

Jokes that have an obvious punchline but your ending is random, unfunny or morbid. An explanation as if you're answering the joke as a question also works.

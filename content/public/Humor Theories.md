There is a multi-disciplinary area of research called "Humour Studies". Smart people wearing gowns and wigs living in the palatial ivory towers of universities have studied and written what humour is about, how it works, why we do it, and how it differs across languages, regions and cultures etc...

I sincerely believe that a deep understanding "jokes" from a cognitive/pragmatic perspective will make one a better joke writer. But part of me is afraid that this is the ultimate frog-killing scenario, and I will destroy my own appreciation of all jokes, forever...

## Frameworks and Theories
- There are many humor theories
- Most are incomplete, they describe a single facet of humor.
- Some academics have attempted to unify all the theories are under one.
	- Usually by broadening one theory to encompass the others.
	- They attempt says more about the author's preferred style of humor than the varied styles of humor of humans.
  		- e.g. Charles R. Gruner - unifies all humour under superiority theory.
	
- In these unified theories, generalization have been made which also encompass events which are not funny, to me personally, but I guess the authors sense of humor varies from mine.
- Some theories are or misleading wrong, as they're outdated and too culturally distant to be relevant today.
- A complete understanding of humour should be multifaceted.

> Laughter results from a pleasant psychological shift

### Social and psychological
1. [Surprise Theory](public/Surprise%20Theory.md) is essential to most humorous events.
2. [Superiority Theory](public/Superiority%20Theory.md) is strongly related to humor in social situations, but isn't essential or sufficient for laughter.
3. [Relief Theory](public/Relief%20Theory.md) laughter arises from the emotional transition from fear/tension to relief/release.

### Humor as a Human Trait
Plato derided laughter as evil, and a loss of control that renders us less than human
> Laughter is an emotion that overrides rational self control -- Plato
> Men of worth must not be represented as overcome by laughter, and still less should we allow such a representation of the gods. -- Plato

To what end would Plato go to avoid an instinctive bodily function?

Darwin described humor (and creativity) as a fundamental characteristic of being human.
> Creativity and humor are two hardwired characteristics of humanbeings (Darwin, 1872/1965; Maslow, 1943

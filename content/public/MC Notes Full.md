## Golden rule:
Have fun!
You are the embodiment of the show, if you’re having fun the audience will too.

## Opening the Show

The MC is one of the most important jobs during a gig. The MC should make the audience feel comfortable, welcome, and part of a special night, the best nights should feel like a party where you meet some really funny new friends

### Introduce yourself
“Hello my name is Chris, I will be your MC for the evening”

### Welcome and thank the crowd
"Welcome everybody, Thank you for coming to the show!"

### Affirm their actions
"You made a great decision to be here tonight!" \

### Build anticipation
Be excited about the whole evening! \
	“We have a great show tonight!” \
	“This is going to be a lot of fun.”

“A brilliant and exciting and outstanding and **awesome **show”

### Jokes
If you tell any jokes in the opening warm up, do your best stuff, don’t do new material

### Admin
Inform the crowd what's going on, for many it’s their first ever show.
Announce the format:
number of acts,
how many breaks etc…
- Amenities - 
- bathrooms,
- drinks ("bartender or bar’s name")
- smoking areas
- fire exits...

You can be funny when doing the "admin”"" but it’s not expected.

### Discourage hecklers
Especially important at open mics, with rookie performers

### Encourage laughter
Some NZ audiences aren’t used to comedy, and need explicit encouragement.

### Warm up

You can do crowd work or material to warm the crowd up.
5-10 minutes of material should be enough on a good night.
If the audience are warmed up, bring the first act on.
Don’t be too funny, as then the other comedians will look shit by comparison.
It’s about giving the other comedians the opportunity, not you.
The audience should get used to laughing, it’s a comedy show after all. 
Start with:

*   Short punchy jokes
*   Family friendly
*   Self-deprecating jokes
*   Clean jokes
*   Crowd work…?

## Introducing Performers
Don't bring on first act to a cold audience, make sure they’re warmed up first.
Try telling a few jokes to get them used to the rhythm of standup comedy.
If they crowd doesn’t get on board, just build excitement for 1st act by making them cheer.

Frame the speaker for the audience.
Establish credibility without over-embellishing
“Always under sell a comedian. Never say: best comedian ever" - Jerry Seinfeld


"Treat the audience as if they’re a new friend, and all the comics appearing on the show are your old friends who you are introducing your new friends to”** - Justin Hamilton ** \
**

"The next comedian is one of the funniest people I know"

**For First Timers**

If it's their first time doing comedy, **tell the crowd.**

Audience will tolerate an awful rookie set, if they understand the context.

“It is difficult to do comedy, takes effort, courage, work”

"Be nice!"

"Be supportive, encouraging, gentle, kind etc”

Do the same if it’s their second time on stage.

"Let’s give them at least 2 good shows before they fuck up"

**For Regular Performers**

Ask the performers if they have personalised introductions.

Semi-scripted banter works wonders for establishing rapport.

 \
Set the tone for upcoming act.

If they’re a clever comedian, tell the audience to listen closely.

If they’re edgy, tell the audience they’re a great guy, but a bit cheeky.

If they’re wild or dark, tell them to “strap themselves in”

**Final tips**

Comedians Name should be the **last **thing you say

“Please give a big round of applause for **Joe Bloggs!!**”

Pronounce their name correctly, ask if you have to

 \
Mic hand off: \
	Put the mic back in the stand before you say their name.

 \
Never leave the stage unattended. 

After announcing their name, step back to give them room to enter. \
Don’t obstruct their stage entrance, it can get a bit awkward. \


Prepare to shake their hand if they offer, but don’t initiate a handshake.

Some comedians will be too focused elsewhere to shake your hand

 

**Returning to the stage**

Remind the audience of the performers name again \
Keep that energy going


    "Keep it going for Joe Bloggs!!"


     

**If last act did well**

don’t do more material, it’s not about you, just introduce the next act. \
There will be time for maybe 1 or 2 witty observations if you thought of any during their act.

**If last act bombed**

Acknowledge last act, without being rude, maybe just a facial expression

Do a couple minutes of material, it won’t take much to get them laughing again

**If last act did SUPER well**

They might be tuckered out from laughing

Reset the energy a little bit, bring them down

The next act doesn’t need to be compared to that

 

**Energy**

**Be Positive and friendly**

**You’re the warm up guy**

You're pumping the crowd up!

Match their energy, but add 20% \
 \
The MC is expected to be upbeat and positive, even if the audience is cold and unreceptive. If you maintain a vibe the audience will eventually join you. \




*   Have fun
*   Build trust
*   Respect the crowd
*   Show interest in them \


Try to make a good first impression, if you get laugh within the first minute GREAT. If you don’t, it’s ok. 

Friendliness and confidence is most important. \
“Great compere not the funniest. Be the kind of person people don’t hate. 

The compere holds it all together so be friendly and nice” - Susan Calman

 \
**Pandering and Faking the energy \
**Even if you’re not in a good mood, act like you are \


Even if it’s a lie, tell them what they want to hear.

“There's no greater honor than performing for you tonight”

“Dunedin audiences are my favourite”

“Many talented and hilarious comedians in the south island”

 \
**Building Energy (through cheering)** \
It’s ok to ask for applause or get a cheer going. \
You can even say that you're “warming the crowd up”. \
 \
If you start cold, enter a lull, or kill the energy… ask for another cheer, or applause for something \
Only do this a few times as repetitive and tiring \
 \
When the audience hears themself cheer it tells everyone (crowd and comedians) that we’re excited for the show. \
 \
**Examples:**


    Cheer if you've been to comedy before


    Cheer if it's your first time


    Let's hear it from the back! \
From the front!


    Now just the men! Now the women


    Mexican Wave \
whistling

**Confidence**

You're in charge, you're the boss. 

The audience will listen to you, trust you, and feel safe, comfortable, and welcomed if you are in command.** **So act like you are in command.

You’re the confident pilot, steering the plane.

No pilot ever says “don’t worry, I know what I’m doing” or “this is my first time”

 \
**Breaking the 4th wall**

The MC is both a comedian **and** audience member. They’re not expected to be a scripted set, but be present in the room and in the moment. \


**Engage with the audience**

Affable - friendly, good natured, easy to talk to.

Flexible - agreeable, willing to listen and adapt

Likable - ???

**Avoid Swearing and negativity**

You are the friendliest comedian

Try not to be too negative, make people feel shit, bullied, etc... \
Keep that mood positive.

**Maintain the energy of the room**

Be energetic. High energy, bravado, loud, excited, confident, enthusiastic.

Talk at a consistent pace, try and avoid awkward pauses.

**Work your arse off on a bad night**

Prepare to eat shit on some nights

It’s about making the show better for the other performers

**Always be light-hearted, cheeky, fun. Positive ribbing**

Cheekiness gives you better range of jokes you can play with. 

Also, backpedaling after insults can work

Apologise, say “just kidding”, then double down straight away.

 

It's amazing what can work. But figure out ways to get energy up, and back up if it falls.

Think about what makes noise, fun.

Consider what improv shows do.

**Warm-up comedian**

A good host/MC shares many skills with a TV audience warm-up comedian.

 



1. Act like you want to be there
2. Do new material for the regulars
3. Don’t' roast, but gentle ribbing is ok
4. Give away candy or other freebies
5. Keep it clean and classy
6. Keep your energy uplifted: caffeine!
7. Pronounce names correctly.



**Crowd Work**

 

Makes the live show a special experience.

 \
Crowd work is different enough from scripted material that the audience won't directly compare you with the other comedians. You don’t even have to be funny, being friendly and open is usually enough.

 \
If the audience isn’t keen on crowd work, fallback to material \


Don’t force the funny, you don’t have to be funny immediately. Keep asking, keep digging till you find the funny. \
The laughs will happen eventually, be patient, don't panic. \


Being genuinely interested in the conversation is more important

Don’t think "where's the joke, where's the joke" \
Try and make a genuine connection, have a friendly chat

They're people. Talk to them. Make eye contact.

Don't fear the crowd. They want to have fun.

Be part of the audience. You're with them!

**Leading Questions**

Yes/No questions are easier to prepare scripted responses for.  \


Who What Where When Why and How questions are far more interesting and can will lead to more spontaneous funniness.

**Address the audience collectively first**

Is anyone here:



*   studying,
*   single, married, got kids
*   local, out of town
*   celebrating: birthday

If anyone responds they are willing to chat, this way you can avoid shy people. \
Read the room, don’t ask an audience of pensioners if they’re students.

**Individuals**

What's your name?

Where are you from

What do you do for work?

What keeps you out of trouble?

Get their name first (especially if you can remember it)

Ask about their day. Segue into prepared material if you can.

Repeat their answers, so everyone can hear, and so you have more time to think of a response

Be prepared for the audience member to be playful, they have a sense of humour, **so listen to them.**

If they crack a joke laugh with them. \
You’ve 100% won if they're comfortable having fun with you.

 \
**Call-back**

Call-backs are gold in crowd work. It’s like a memory game. Remember people’s names, jobs, etc… but also referencing previous comedian’s jokes is always a good move. \
If you can remember everything they say it gives you more to play with.

Icebreakers, jazzed up and more fun. Team-building.

People start talking in the breaks because they know stuff about each other. \
 \
**Canned material \
**If you’re speaking with a builder and have a joke about a builder, tell that joke. \
You can build up a repertoire of canned lines.

If you don't know anything about their job \
	“what's the biggest thing you've ever built?  \
Sometimes awkward (if they’re a midwife or something) but usually fun. \


**Improv/play**

Tell them what they are (after asking)

e.g. if they are a hairdresser, TELL them they work for house of drag.

Agree with them. Funnier the more obvious it is that it's a lie.

“I'm pregnant” - "Oh cool, same" "What a coincidence, me too!"

Playful teasing (don’t put them down).

Don't attack their identity or anything important.

Not anything unchangeable

Tease yourself too.

Tease, push, pull.

It's like puppies play fighting - don't draw blood.

 

**Flirty Jokes**

Flirting is fun and friendly. shine spotlight on one person, they'll laugh.

Usually well received, because of the context of the show, you're not sincerely hitting on anyone. It's just harmless fun.

Be nice

 

Talk to people that look interesting.

Always talk to couples (Hacky maybe, but people love it)

Pointing at people and saying "tinder couple". People will laugh at the idea.

Couples will disagree on something, or not know an answer, or answer too soon… and an awkward situation between a romantic couple is funny

**Backpedaling **(getting into really uncomfortable territory)

Even yell "backpeddddalllll".

Happens with couples sometimes. Stuff that you shouldn't be talking about in public, uncomfortable, panicky, etc. Move away from them quickly.

Learn to pick up on those signals, but don't call it too soon. Other people have to have picked up on it. Make victim feel safe that you won't pry too much more, but make audience feel like you're still having cheeky digs into it. Don't try too hard.

It's not about the MC, not about always being funny.

It's about them, and the shared experience.

 

**Hecklers**

Don't overreact. Stay calm.

 \
Be **authoritative **with the crowd if they step out of line, if people are chatting during the acts - tell them this is out of order in as **fun and as authoritatively** as you can \
 

Mirror - repeat what they say

Inviting - 

Containing - defuse the situation

Handle nicely at first, get progressively meaner as audience gets on your side. Shit on them more and more, but audience has to also hate them.

Can shit on them, and then do an aside to rest of audience referencing  it.

This is esp important when it's just nice chiming in. Have to do gently, or shut up some other way. Distract them.

Get different kinds with different desires. Attention, vs just one quip from anonymity. So maybe try actually engaging them, but that might stoke, so change it.

Maybe try to draw the hecklers to you? Sometimes it works, esp for that gig, but encourages in future, says it's ok.

Sometimes doesn't have to be clever.

Free-source heckler retorts exist too (generally free shared, no one wants comics to lose to hecklers).

Physically approaching can be aggressive signal.

If really bad:

Bar or promoter should kick them out. Talk to staff if you need to. Tell person that they will be 

kicked out. If staff won't kick them out, tell them they'll have to MC the show.

Rarely, it will be helping with the show. But in general, don't want to support that idea that hecklers are "making the show better" like lots of them think.

**People/tables that just talk**:

Disturbing to comedian, audience doesn't consciously listen as much. Distracting.

Hard to deal with.

Even just tell audience that you are going to stare at them.

Even just little insult/observations, but not mean. Benign stereotypes.

Or apologise to them for putting a show on during their conversation.

 

Be condescending when audience is on your side. Winds them up heaps, but they can't respond. Pulls rug out. Overdo the laugh, etc.

 

"You know, you are allowed to enjoy this. You've done that before, yeah?"

**Closing**

Be brief, be thankful, be grateful.

 

Thank bar staff, all the acts

Promote social media We have facebook, instagram, twitter, grindr

Plug future shows

Make people feel as if they participated in something special, a moment.

Thank crowd work participants by name.

Support the arts, support local talent

Grass roots comedy right here

Ground level stuff

Make them feel like the heroes for coming out, because they are.

Finish with genuine, heartfelt speech at the end to get people to support, come back, grow the scene.

**Backstage etiquette** 



*   Not technically your job, but talk to comics that look like they want to be talked to. "Greenrooming". Make them feel good, relaxed.
    *   Same with audience members, as they walk in.
    *   Get audience to know you, feel on-board.

**Common mistakes** \
Don’t steal the show. It’s not about the MC, it’s about the other acts. \
The MC shouldn’t be the crowd favourite. \
 \
Don’t launch straight into a scripted routine, start by welcoming the crowd. Many audience members have never been to live stand-up, tell them what to expect. \
 \
DO NOT introduce the first act if you’re not getting laughs. \
Keep pushing, the audience will warm up to you, eventually.

Don't do material after the headliner



Producing and Promoting

 

Try to stay ~90 mins for full show, any longer and people get tired and bored.

Close each half with a strong comedian

Promote well and put on a good show = they'll come back

People will only give you their money once if it's shit. Does masses of damage doing a shit gig.

Also make prices relative to what others around you do.

**Organising the lineup**

Start strong, end strong.

Positive and punch goes first. Should be your 4th best, roughly.

Close half with your second best. Then 3rd best to open 2nd half, then close with best act.

Organising order:

Depends, more for promoter.

Pro show, doesn't matter as much, everyone should make it.

But in general, someone positive and punchy should go first. Don't want to offend and turn people off first.

1st half opener: 3rd Best Comedian

1st half closer: 2nd Best Comedian

--break--

2nd half opener: 4th Best Comedian

2nd half closer: Best Comedian

(this all depends on styles, of course).

# What is the "Anti Joke"
- Missclassified Anti jokes
	- The most famous *"anti-joke"* is [[The Chicken Joke]], which is actually a pun.
	- Sometimes meta-jokes or self-referential jokes are mislabeled as anti-jokes.
- The conventional understanding of an “Anti-jokes” is an unfunny joke.
- Anti-jokes can be funny for the same reason any jokes is funny, they’re surprising.
- Most jokes employ subversion for surprise, some clever twist;
- The anti-joke however does not employ subversion.
- Instead of something unexpected we get something blindingly obvious.
- often by giving a basic and factual answer to a setup.

> Q: How do you get a nun pregnant?
> A: You fuck her

> Q: What do you call a black man who flies a plane?
> A: a pilot

> Q: What do you call a doctor who graduates bottom of his class in medical school?
> A: Doctor

- The joke listener expects some clever way to seduce a nun or something racially charged, but instead gets the obvious. Therein lies the misdirection. Expecting the unexpected to be unknown but finding it instead to be well known indeed.
- The anti-joke’s surprise comes not from subversion but from our own obliviousness to the obvious.
- The expectation of some new information is subverted by providing nothing new.

> When I came home I expected a surprise and there was no surprise for me, so, of course, I was surprised - Ludwig Wittgenstein

> This is the secret to a long marriage. Don't get divorced. - Japanese Proverb

- Expect either sincere marital advice or a glib remark.
	- Never leave the room angry
	- copious amounts of wine and adultery
- Something factual and obvious.
	- Not getting divorced is implicitly entailed by the expectation.

# Tautology
- Some anti jokes are [Tautology](Tautology.md)
	- The tautology is the most logically rigorous way to construct an antijoke.
	- Which is a statement that is true by necessity of its logical form.
	- Essentially say the same thing twice.
	- The wet water is wet.
	- Water is wet.
	- Two plus two is four
	- 2 + 2 = 4
- It ain’t over till it’s over – Yogi Berra
	- The setup is the same as the punchline, usually in different words.
- Every sixty seconds in Africa, a minute passes
- Sometimes the punchline isn't semantically identical, but is still entailed by the premise.
- Be yourself, everyone else is taken – Oscar Wilde
- You know, if you lived here you'd be home by now. - Lionel Hutz
- You can tell a lot about one's personality from what they're like - Harry Hill
- It’s a fight to the finish, that’s a good place to end it - Mitch Hedberg
- Your life feels empty, because your life is empty - Dylan Moran
- If it’s not a tautology, it is still stating the obvious.
- The key is to deliver the joke as if it weren’t obvious, but rather a profound revelation
- The secret to a long life, is not dying early
- 50% of students perform below the national average
- Teen pregnancy drops off significantly after age 25

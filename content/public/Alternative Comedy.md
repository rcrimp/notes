## History of Alternative Comedy Movement(s)
- 1920 - 1960-ish
	- In the beginning "Stand up Comedy" was the alternative performing art
		- Mainstream performances: music and dance
	- Standup comedy as an art form evolved these musical performance spaces
		- musical hall, burlesque, vaudeville, minstrel...
	- The "emcee" would entertain the audience between the acts with jokes
	- Comedians would "share" jokes, the idea of joke ownership didn't yet exist
	- Eventually the "Comedian" would perform their own act perform alongside singers/dancers/etc.. 
		- sometimes alone, or as a comedy duo (Laurel and Hardy) 
	- The jokes were slapstick, mockery, sexual content "low brow"
		- Many jokes were sexist racist, homophobic etc.. i.e. street jokes.
- 1960s
	- [Lenny Bruce](Lenny%20Bruce.md) was alternative to the mainstream
		- specificlly doing anti-racism, anti-homophobia material
- 1970-1980
	- The first *"alternative comedy scene"* in the UK established ground rules against racist sexist jokes.
	- Performing original material was the third rule, you could not perform jokes you didn't write yourself.
	- It became a more honest expression of self, rather than pandering to what the audience wanted.
- 1980-1990
	- Story telling, anecdote driven performances from comedians
	- Bill Cosby, Billy Connolly
- 1990
	- Observational comedy

## Today
- Alternative comedy tends to become mainstream over time. 
- The style of the “alternative comedians” of previus eras are mainstream nowadays. Original jokes, inoffensive material, sharing personal experience...

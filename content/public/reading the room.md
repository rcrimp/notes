# Self Sabotage
- never tell the audience the gig is going badly
	- it will ruin the vibe
	- they will believe you
	- nobody wants to hear a pilot saying
		- *"this isn't going well"*
- an off hand [[saving lines]] is okay
	- it doesn't ruin the vibe to point out a ***SINGLE*** joke needs work, that shows self awareness and humility
	- never say ***the entire*** set, or entire show is going bad
- don't ask WHY the vibe is bad
	- Instead ask:- _What is this room telling me it wants right now?_

## Gauging the room Early
- easy way to gauge the room, just ask!
	- *who's been to comedy before?*
- best done within the first few minutes
	- litmus test for expected awkwardness
	- how much hand-holding and crowd work needed to warm up

# Easy Vibe Improvers
- build positive energy naturally
	- ask for a ==cheer==
	- cheer for the bar, etc..
	- make some noise for... etc..
	- who's having a good night
- build anticipation
	- who's ready for a good night?
- The audience want to be excited
	- so, let them be excited
	- give them an opportunity
	- give them permission express it loudly!

# Be Reassuring
- if the vibe feels low
	- usually early in the night
	- an odd tension, nerves
	- or less laughter than usual
- the solutions in two parts:
	1. **acknowledge** the tension
	2. **reassure** the crowd it'll be fine
- example
	- I know many of you haven't been to an open mic before, and you're a bit uncertain about it, but you've got nothing to worry about.
- IMPORTANT
	- don't accept the blame
	- don't label the gig as "bad"
	- you aren't a BAD comedian doing BADLY
	- the crowd is simply nervous, this early in the gig
	- a nervous vibe just needs time to settle

> By the way... I can sense that for a few of you this is probably you're last thing that you're going to see at the Fringe, and you've saved it up, and you're like... "uhh just come one..." I just want to say, It's gonna be fine, this is fucking awesome. You genuinely do not have to lower expectations. - Daniel Kitson

## Crowd Work
- small gigs suffer from a special tension
	- awkwardness, discomfort, uncertainty
	- self conscious
	- close proximity to strangers
	- in an unfamiliar setting
- crowd work can resolve this tension
	- ask genuine questions
	- make friends with several people
	- make them feel comfortable
	- and everyone else will too as there are now fewer strangers in the room
- intended vibe
	- house party of friends

## Convincing
- a bad comedian bragging about themselves is unconvincing
- the MC is in a better position to be reassuring
	- a trusted third party
	- the MC has authority and status
	- less perceived conflict of interest

## Other areas to explore
- body language in a quiet room
	- are they still engaged?
		- smiles, eye contact
	- or are they averting their gaze
		- looking down, at phones, at eachother
- laughter types
	- early in a gig laughter is less reliable before trust is established
	- scattered
	- delayed hesitant
	- rolling - trust esablished
- high energy isn't always good
	- loud and drunk crowds can be worse than quiet shy crowds
	- drunked cheering at random points
	- talking during setups
	- these people want to party not listen
	- take control early, get their attention with crowd work

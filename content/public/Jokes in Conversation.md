## Jokes in Conversation
Joke telling is a game usually played in monologue, by way of the speaker telling a joke.
Most of our daily humour occurs in conversation. Which is why many streets jokes begin as a question, or some other speech act which demands a response.

- Why did the…?
- What’s the difference… ?
- Knock, knock...

Jokes in conversation flouting Grice’s maxims
the deliberate violation of the Cooperative Principle; flout one of Grice's conversational maxims.

> Alice: Can you call me a taxi?
> Irene: OK, you're a taxi!


Here the ambiguity of the verb *to call* is exploited for comedic effect.

> Alice: Do you have the time?
> Irene: Yes.

Again, Irene deliberately misunderstands the question, there is an obvious Speech Act, an indirect demand: `tell me the time` she instead chooses to answer the literal interpretation of the question. We call this behaviour Playing Dumb, Feigning Ignorance, Selective Obliviousness, Playing Possum}, and being Deliberately Obtuse. Here is another example of Irene acting in an uncooperative manner after entering a restaurant

>  Waiter: Are you here for dinner?
>  Irene: No, I am here to steal your plates.

The waiter's could be expressing a formality of the restaurant setting, or perhaps they're attempting to determine whether Irene wants a meal or just a drink so as to choose an appropriate seating location for her. Either way, Irene chooses to admit her desire for criminal activity instead! an inappropriate admission, clearly misguided or an outrageous lie. A dull waiter might be fooled by her sharp wit, ideally however, they'd acknowledge the absurdity of the crockery crime confession and laugh in surprise!

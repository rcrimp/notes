# Antithesis
> Repetition by negation "A man should be mourned at his birth, not his death"
> - Arthur Quinn

> Personally, I would never claim to be a good conversationalist, though I do regularly prove it.

An antithesis is a kind of parallel structure that provides a sense of semantic opposition. This sense is ill-defined, which makes them hard to classify.

Their scheme could be isocolon, parison, ...

The listener being able to predict the second parallelism is essential.

1: opposing ideas can be contrasted or combined into an antithesis.

2: Parallel grammatical structure, containing contrasting ideas.
  
## Examples

> Float like a butterfly, sting like a bee

> That's one small step for a man, one giant leap for mankind  

> The ability to play chess is the sign of a gentleman.

> The ability to play chess well is the sign of a wasted life - Paul Morphy ~ 1850

### Oscar Wilde
Oscar Wilde used many antithesis, especially in his novel Dorian Gray. Oscar’s antithesis are both clever and they give the sense of elucidating a deeper meaning.

> No civilized man ever regrets a pleasure, and no uncivilized man ever knows what a pleasure is.

> Let it be for good, not for evil.

> Don’t tell me that you have exhausted life. When a man says that one knows that life has exhausted him.

> Nowadays, all the married men live like bachelors, and all the bachelors like married men.

> When a woman marries again it is because she detested her first husband. When a man marries again, it is because he adored his first wife.
 
> Men always want to be a woman's first love; women want to be a man's last romance.

> When we are happy, we are always good, but when we are good, we are not always happy. - (The Picture of Dorian Gray, 1891)

> We teach people how to remember, we never teach them how to grow. - ("The Critic as Artist," 1991)

> Wherever there is a man who exercises authority, there is a man who resists authority. - (The Soul of Man Under Socialism, 1891)
 
> Society often forgives the criminal; it never forgives the dreamer. - ("The Critic as Artist," 1991)

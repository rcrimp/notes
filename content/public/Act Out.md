---
title: Act Out
---

Acting out to a story to visualizes, to explain, to re-enforce

- Playing pretend
- Mimicking

## Types
- Expressed with verbal cues
	- "then they said"
- Expressed with voice
	- accent
	- any change in tone / Intonation
- Expressed with mime
	- physically act out an action

## Impressions
- The simplest and most common in pre-90s standup is the *"impression"*
	- State the celebrity (or character)
		- state their position or situation
	- Act out the situation in their voice
- Zach Galifianakis
- Jim Carrey
- Seth MacFarlane

## Characterization
- Some comedians adopt a persona called a character
- The act out
	- can be thought of as an Instant Character
- You can act out a stereotype or a real person

## Personification
- Be a different person. accents and tone
- Take both sides of a conversation
- Can use the voice of another person to mock them.

- Expressing a stronger emotional force
- Eddie Izzard - acts out conversations

## Depicting An Event
- The Act Out is often used by story tellers to communicate an interaction between people in a story.
- BIlly Connolly
- Robin Williams
- Daniel Kitson
- However one-liner comedians can also use this technique to great effect
> I think they named oranges before they named carrots. 'What are these?' 'Those are orange: oranges.' 'What about these?' 'Oh, shit. Long pointies? We'll go by shape now? - Demitri Martin
- See how a single idea can be expanded into multiple punchlines with an act out.
- Great examples include
	- Paul F Tompkins peanut brittle routine.
	- Gary Gulman abbreviating the states

## Anthropomorphic Personification
- You can even personify non-animate objects
- Hannibal Buress
	- personifies: his testicles, syphilis
- Ross Noble, Rory Scovel, Dylan Moran, Maria Bamford

## Imitation
- Satire, Parody, Mock, Ridicule
- Exaggerated position for comic effect.
- overstate a perceived truth for comic effect
- overstate a contrary position for comic effect
- Adopt the opposite
- Intended to mock and ridicule

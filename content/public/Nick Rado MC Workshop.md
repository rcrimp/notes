# Nick Rado
Hosting a comedy show is a craft, it’s different to stand-up.
It’s about the whole show not the individual acts.
Best way to learn is practicing in smaller rooms, small crowds, 3-10 people.
3 tiers of MC: 
1. Basic
2. Intermediate
3. Expert

If you manage the basics, you’ve done a good job.
Don’t worry about anything else, just nail all the basics.


## 1: Basic
It's not about being funny, it's about being informative.

### Before the show
Get the room ready for the show. 
Get some black sheets / curtains to cover doorways.
Minimize distractions
- Turn off lights
- TVs
- fans
- anything moving, etc…

### Seating
Very important to reduce the distance between the performer and the "crowd".
So arrange the chairs close to the stage, all facing the stage.

If the front row(s) are empty invite them forward.
If you have a small crowd, arrange them into a semi circle around the stage.
To encourage people to move forward, offer them something:
- offer them applause
- offer immunity from being picked on
- offer free drinks (gets expensive)

The easiest people to move forward are your friends and fellow comedians

### Schedule
Introduce yourself, then the show structure:

> I’m the MC, we have 4 acts, then a break, then 3 more acts in the second half”

If people need to pee, and they don’t know that a break is coming they will become distracted.

### Audience Liberties
Tell the audience what they can't do, and what they should.

#### Hecklers
Tell them not to heckle other acts
> please direct all heckles to me
> You wouldn’t bring your own sandwiches to a restaurant

#### Clapping
Tell them to clap if they like something.
> the more love you give, the better the show will be

#### Laughing
Tell them it’s ok to laugh out loud
> it’s not TV, this is LIVE!

#### Phones 
Turn off your phones

#### Venue Amenities
Point out the location of the amenities.
1. Bathroom
2. Smoking area
3. Bar, etc... 

### Silence

Some audiences will be very quiet initially.  \
The MC should take the bullet and endure the bomb with a smile. 

It could take until the second act before the audience loosen up.

Get these basics sorted, and it’ll be a brilliant show


## 2: Intermediate Tier

people want to escape their lives, they want distraction from their problems

get their focus engaged on the stage, get them out of their heads.

Do this by creating energy, excitement, anticipation...


### Energy

Make them cheer or applaud for something

“Give it up for Tuesday!!”

“Let’s hear it Dunedin”

“Go crazy cos it’s the weekend”

“Make some noise if you’re here to have fun!!!”

The audience will associate the MC with excitement, it’s pavlovian, it works.

When one person in the audience hears the rest of the crowd’s excitement they in turn will become excited. Hysteria is highly contagious.


### Crowd Work

Don’t worry about being funny, you’re fact finding for the other acts. 

Asking the audience as a collective helps find people who aren’t shy

**Collective Questions**


    “Whos’ been to comedy before?”


    “Who here is from overseas?”


    “Who here is in a relationship?”


    “Who here is single?”


    “Who here is a student?”

**Individual Questions**


    “What’s your name?”


    “What do you do?”

"What keeps you out of trouble?"

**Leading Questions**

You can ask leading questions to enter small pieces of your own material

	“Who’s your favourite Spice Girl?... Well mine is… [material]”

You can ask leading questions for other comedian’s material

It’ll make their time on stage easier if they can immediately segue into material relevant to someone in the audience.


### Introducing Acts

remember the acts name

TELL THE AUDIENCE TO APPLAUD, don’t suggest, order them to. \
say the comedians name last

Side note: If the MC has done well you can attach yourself to their success by saying: “How good was Justine? she’s brilliant”


### Elephant(s) in the room

If there’s a TV on, mention it. If there’s something obvious in the room like extravagant decor, mention it.


## 3: Expert Tier



1. Build energy first! Make them applaud and cheer for something
2. Start fact finding from the crowd, talk to them, start a conversation
3. Show admin:
    1. show structure, breaks...
    2. encourage clapping and laughing
    3. discourage heckling
    4. toilets, phones, etc...
4. Bring on the first act


### Being Funny \
Only after you’re confident on the basic / intermediate stuff, time to get funny. \
Write jokes for each announcement, e.g:

“We’re filming tonight’s show, so our apologies if you’re in witness protection”

“Turn off your phone, unless it has the ringtone of a possum”


### Expert Crowd Work

Your primary goal is still to find facts for the other acts.

Speak to 3 people, spend a few minutes on each person \
Use your material as backup if crowd work doesn't work

If they’re obviously lying e.g. “I’m a outer-space dolphin trainer” back off

**Characters**


    Turn your “fact machines” into characters


    Think of them as stereotypes or cartoons


    Give them a back story and motivation

**Find connections between characters:**


    e.g. one find two people who are single, or even ask a couple if they’re interested in a threesome with the single person

**Keep coming back to your characters**

e.g. if they’ve been in a relationship for 3 years, after the first act, ask

“Still together? Yes? Now it’s 3 years and 10 minutes, keep it up!”


### Expert Introductions

Ask the comedian for a personalised introduction, something they might use to naturally segue into material. 

Some comedians might have awards, tv spots, or previous experience you could announce to establish credibility.


### After the break

Thank them for coming back

Reset the room so they’re all paying attention

Settle any drunk people

Minimize the chatter

Do material when opening the second half.  \
Get the crowd into listening mode, not banter mode. \
The first act after the break is usually new and the crowd should get into the rhythm of a prepared monologue not interacting.


### Closing The Show

Thank the venue

Plugging future shows, and social media channels.

Invite the audience to hang out and chat with the comedians by the bar

i.e. push drink sales and build audience following

Finally thank the true heroes of the show….: “Tim, Alice the Karen” 

i.e. the 3 audience members you’ve been speaking to during the show.


### Removing People

In the rare case there’s an intrusive person ruining the gig, give them 3 strikes.

Heckler put downs are usually insult roasts or condescension.

You can use classic put downs first:

[Heckler Put Downs](Heckler%20Put%20Downs.md)

	Giving sailors blowjobs, etc..

Third strike, when even the audience are tired of the heckler

“hands up who wants to keep this guy in the show”

When nobody puts their hand up, say:

“Mate… even you didn’t put your hand up”

Don’t accuse the heckler of ruining the show for the comedians

be clear that they’re ruining the show for the paying audience

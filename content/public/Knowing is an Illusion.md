# Knowing
One does not choose to believe the blueness of sky
One "knows" the sky is blue as one "knows" the divine to be true.

"knowing" is an emotional experience, certainty is an illusion of the mind.
"understanding" is also an hallucination.

You cannot choose to believe the sky is "green". When you haven't observed it.

What if we observe a green sky?
If our observations conflict with our thoughts?
if an incongruency arises, between the observed reality and our internal world?

1. Change your thoughts to become congruent with the observation.    
2. Discredit the observation.  
  
Discrediting the observation (2) is cognitively easier
- An exception that proves the rule
- A hallucination of the mind
- An elaborate hoax
- A mass conspiracy
- A paradigm shift of the world and society is easier to believe, than believing that our thoughts have been wrong our whole lives.  
  
A single observation is rarely sufficient to change a mind.  

Our thoughts (ideology) are built from repeated patterns of observation. These patterns are built one at a time from birth, unfortunately mental heuristics often cause category mistakes (e.g. temporal association => causality). We have also been taught doctrine that to assist categorization of observations. Doctrine too can be faulty.  
  
The belief in misinformation.  
Every time a believer falsely categorizes evidence for X, it's added to the implicit stack of evidence for X. Then, when one stumbles upon a single clue against the existence of X, it cannot compete against the compendium of false belief.

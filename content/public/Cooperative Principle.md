The Cooperative Principle of conversation suggests we follow implicit rules to be understood. - Paul Grice

## 4 Maxims of conversation
- Quality
	- Honest and truthful
	- Don’t speak falsely
	- Don’t speak without evidence
- Quantity
	- provide enough information
	- and not too much
	- brevity
- Relation (relevance)
	- Be relevant
- Manner (clarity)
	- Orderly
	- Cleary and concise
		- Avoid obscurity
		- Avoid ambiguity


Alternatively
1. Make your contribution as informative as is required for the current purposes of the exchange.
2.	Do not make your contribution more informative than is required.
3.	Do not say what you believe to be false.
4.	Do not say that for which you lack adequate evidence.
5.	Avoid obscurity of expression.
6.	Avoid ambiguity.
7.	Be brief.
8.	Be orderly.

## Flouting the Maxims
- Irony and Metaphor break the maxim of Quality as "literal" mis truths
- Some believe that all verbal jokes flout (at least one of) the 4 maxims 
- But flouting a maxims isn't always a joke
> From a pragmatic point of view, the cause of funniness of verbal jokes is the manipulation of [Implicature](public/Implicature.md) and [[Entailment]]. The speaker deliberately violates or flouts Grice's conversational maxims to create unexpected or non-natural entailment - Delia Chiaro
- Break a maxim to implicate. 
- 
- Implicature
	- - intended interpretations
- Implication
	- - assumptions actually inferred. Possibly accurate or inaccurate.

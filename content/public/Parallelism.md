Parallel structure: Two (or more) phrases that are similar in grammatical form.

Parallel structures are used for emphasis, usually emphasising a contrast between two concepts, or two aspects of the same concept.

### Poetic Repetition
Parallelism is much like repetition however, with parallelism some features are held constant (invariants) while others features change (variants). Invariants are usually syntax, prepositions, articles, pronouns; invariants are usually nouns phrases, verbs, adjectives...

Much like repetition, parallelism can be used for rhythmic reasons aiding memorisation.

## Tricolon
[Rule of 3](public/Rule%20of%203.md)
The rule of 3 can be described as a kind of parallelism. The first two repetitions have minimal variants, the third having the most.**

## transfer association
Parallel structures can imbue associations:

## Expectations
the repeated structure allows the listener to guess the second phrase, the punchline.

Similar to how the rule of three builds the expectation of the third, a good isocolon can establish those expectations allowing for an aposiopesis.

### Parison
parison, parisosis, membrum, compar

Parallelism where the second clause is dependent.

> an even gait of sentences answering each other in measures interchangeably. - John Hoskins (1599)

Examples

> The closer you get, the better you look

> Everything you want, nothing you don't

> The more you try to control things, the less you will control.
> The harder you pursue someone, the further they will retreat.

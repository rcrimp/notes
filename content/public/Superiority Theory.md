## Cruel Laughter 
Evidence suggests that the dominant form of laughter in pre-civilization socities is cruel-laughter, being amused by acts of cruelty.

It seems wildly inappropriate these days to laugh at the vicious and gory death of another, but there exists little evidence that our ancestors laughed for any other reason.
1. Iliad
2. The Bible
Cruelty was once the only form of amusement.

Animals appear to amuse themselves with cruelty. Cats playing with helpless mice etc.

## Fall of Cruelty 
As society has evolved over the past few thousand years, so to has the role of laughter. The rise of empathy, the sensitivity of the suffering of others has made cruel-laughter inappropriate in many spaces. Although we need only observe the behaviour of children to note the pervasive occurrence of cruelty as a form of amusement.

Superiority Theory may have once been a comprehensive explanation for laughter, but nowadays a sense of superiority is neither necessary nor sufficient to provoke laughter anymore.

However, it is important to consider the inverse: feeling inferior prevents laughter. One is very unlikely to laugh if they feel inferior, if they feel threatened, challenged, or if their social status is brought into question.

It's easy to conclude that a neutral (or superior) disposition is required for laughter to take place. There are exceptions, for example [nervous laughter](nervous%20laughter.md)

## History
Plato and Aristotle both agreed that laughter is the emotion of scorn

> Laughter is malign evil ... to take delight in another's self-ignorance -- Plato

This is easier to understand when considering what we know of Ancient Greek culture. Specifically, the professional comedy playwrights Aristophanes and Euripides wrote jokes about Ugliness, Stupidity, Drunkenness, Obesity, Baldness, Farts, Sexual-Innuendo and Phalli, and if their work is an accurate representation of the common humour in Ancient Greece then Plato's opinion is understandable. Especially when you know that Socrates was lampooned in the Aristophanes's play, The Clouds.

Cruel Mockery, Teasing, Ridicule and Roasting are tools of the Bully... and the Satirist.

Despite public appreciation for this form of humor, academics across history have maintained a dismissive attitude towards it. Here's Descartes

> people with very obvious defects such as those who are lame, blind of an eye, hunched-backed, or who have received some public insult, are specially given to mockery' --- Descartes, The Passions of the Soul 1649

In the late 17th century, superiority theory was developed further by Hobbes

> Laughter expresses a sudden glory arising from some conception of some eminency in ourselves, by comparison with the infirmity of others, or with our own formerly--- Hobbes

# Paralogical Devices
- The comedian discovers some spurious association or coincidental correlation
- The comedian then provides a logic structure to justify a plausible connection.
- Sometimes sound, sometimes unsound. 

## Circular logic
> My belt holds my pants up, but the belt loops hold my belt up. I don't really know what's happening down there. Who is the real hero? - Mitch Hedberg

- Paralogism Jokes are recognised similar to Verbal irony, hyperbole, understatement. 
- The speaker speaks a deviation from the norm, the listener laughs in acknowledging the irrational thought over the expected rational thought.
- Some Paralogism may require more cognitive strain to unravel, as they are riddles / brain teasers

# Abductive Reasoning
Often times the joke writing process works backwards, from the consequent-punchline to the antecedent-premise, through unsound abductive reasoning.

- Deductive reasoning
	- deduce conclusions from given premises

- Inductive reasoning
	- assume pattern are rules

> Everyone who went blind in adulthood, probably had at least one wank

> Milk is the gateway drug to heroin, as all heroin addicts started life drinking Milk.

- Abductive reasoning 
	- abduce premises from given conclusions
“have you ever seen me and Jesus in the same room together?... didn’t think so”

## Affirming the Consequent
- if X then Y. I observe Y therefore X.

> The only physical description of Jesus is found in Luke 2:21, describing his circumcision...

## Denying the antecedent
- If X then Y. I observe not X therefore not Y.

> I can’t be a terrorist, I’m not Muslim”

- Sometimes a paralogic fallacy can be interpreted as offensive, not humourous.
	- Is the previous statement a joke?
- A rational agent would infer that the speaker believes that only Muslims can be terrorists.
- Or possibly through catagorical fallacy, “all terrorists are Muslim”
- Either way, an untrue (and offensive) proposition.

## Street Jokes
### Spider Legs
> Renowned scientist working in obscurity OR a young boy
> First experiment proves a spider can follow verbal commands:
> “Jump”, “Walk left”, etc...
> The spider's legs are then removed, and then no longer manages to follow commands
> The second experiment proves that once you remove a spider's legs, it goes deaf.

### Professor of Logic
> Random stranger OR new neighbour is asked what he does for a living.
 “I am a professor of logic", he struggles to explain what that entails so he uses an example.
> I see you have a dog house out back.
> By that I deduce that you have a dog.
> The fact that you have a dog, Leads me to deduce that you have a family.
> Since you have a family I deduce that you have a wife
> since you have a wife, I can deduce that you are heterosexual
> That is deductive reasoning
> Later on, the protagonist tries to describe how logic works to a friend 
> Let me explain with an example. Do you have a dog house?
> No
> You must be homosexual then.

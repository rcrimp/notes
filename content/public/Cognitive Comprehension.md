# Cognitive Comprehension
Figure of Speech -> Comprehension of Conflict -> Mental Shift -> Emotional Effect

A spoken joke is constructed from language.
A joke is independent from language as it be translated into different words.
The most prominent example is Eddie Izzard performing her show 'Wunderbar' in English, German, and French.
However, a joke can also be expressed using different words in the same language.

1. I used to do drugs. I still do, but I used to too. - Mitch Hedberg
2. I used to do drugs, but that was way back there. *gestures back stage* - Dave Attell
3. I haven't done drugs in 3, maybe 4 ...*pause*... minutes - Justin Rusty White
4. The old me was reliant on these drugs. I needed to be distracted from the interminable suffering of it all. But I've grown as a person since this morning.

The 4 jokes above use different words, but rely on the same cognitive mechanism for humour.
Misdirection, a presupposition is invalidated.
The presupposition? usually, a declaration of ending drug used means to quit permanently, for health reasons, rehabilitation etc.

The cognitive mechanism happens during the comprehension of the joke.
The comprehension involves some form of mental conflict.

## Figures of Speech
A figure of speech are grammatical. Oftentimes deviations from regular speech.
Choosing a word that sounds similar to a previous word, rhyming trope.
Reordering a phrase that has the same syntax as a previous phrase. Parallelism scheme.

| figure of speech | lexical level | example        |
| ---------------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| trope            | word          | rhyme          |
| scheme           | phrase        | chiasmus       |
| joke             | sentence      | paraprosdokian |

A figure of speech is independent from the joke (as the choice of words is).
You can express the same joke using different figures of speech.

A paradox is not a figure of speech, it is a sensation experience when comprehending a sentence.
A figure of speech is a vehicle to communication the sensation

## Emotional Effect
The intended result of a joke is to cause a sufficient mental shift or emotional change to elicit laughter.
Surprise is the the most common base emotion, but is often accompanied with another(s).
1. Surprise
2. Disbelief / Incredulity
3. Shock (surprise + transgression)
4. Awe (surprise + realization)
5. Archimedean Eureka (surprise + insight)
9. Flattery (superiority)
10. Bewilderment (surprise + confusion)

The mental shift must harmless.
Watching your best friend die will cause some a mental shift and emotional effect, but not amusement or laughter.
But watching a hated movie villain die might could amusement.
A harmful mental shift may cause us to take immediate action, or to cry.
	No action must be required to preserve our safety or survival.


## Conflict and Harmony
Most jokes rely on some conflict.
The conflict does not exist in the words, but in the comprehension of the words. 

For example. An ironic event is conflict between expectations and reality.

%%The the simultaneous presence of conflict and harmony%%

The stronger the conflict, and the stronger the harmony, the more surprising, and funnier the joke.

## Comprehension of Conflict and Mental Shift(s)
Before the emotional effect is experienced, the comprehension happens.

Comprehension of a Conflict that results in some mental shift.

Varying levels verbal, conceptual, emotional, sometimes many levels simultaneously.
Personally, my verbal-comprehension appears to occur before the emotional or conceptual levels.
So I rank those at the top, the most shallow

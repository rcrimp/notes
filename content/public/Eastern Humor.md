## Western views are positive
Western views of humour include [Relief Theory](public/Relief%20Theory.md), that humor releases tension, fear, and anxieties.
It's a social tool to help overcome uncomfort and awkwardness

Humor as a trait is desirable, affable
(Allport, 1937; Maslow, 1968; Mintz, 1983; Mindess et al., 1985)

Humorous people are more attractive
(e.g., Regan and Joshi, 2003; Fraley and Aron, 2004)

Humorous people are more motivating, creative, capable
(e.g., Sternberg, 1985; Priest and Swain, 2002)

Humorous people are more mentally healthy, self-aware, well-adjusted
(Allport, 1961; Martin and Ford, 2018).

## Eastern views are negative
Humor and making jokes is disrespectful and not serious.
View originates in Confucianism (similar to Plato's views)

Humor is not a desirable trait in Chinese Culture (Rudowicz and Yue, 2002; Yue, 2011)
Expect from  people who are "experts"

being funny jeopardizes social status
(Bond, 1996; Liao, 1998, 2007; Yue, 2010)

Humor isn't evidence of creativity
(e.g., Rudowicz and Yue, 2011; Yue and Hui, 2011, 2015)

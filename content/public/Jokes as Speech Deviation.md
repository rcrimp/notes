## Speech Conventions
Which came first, the grammar textbook or the grammatically correct speaker?
By age 9 a native language speaker is usually fluent, implicitly aware of all language conventions, able to form grammatically complex and accurate speech without ever glancing once at a grammar textbook.

## Speech Deviations
What is humourous is what is surprising, and what is surprising is the unexpected deviation from conventional speech. Sometimes breaking a grammar rule, more often breaking a different speech convention. This is one of the reasons why the verbal blunder of a child is often humorous.

- I goed more faster
- You're a grown up, I'm a grown down
- My mum looks beautifully, your mum looks uglily

I was able to witness my little sister grow up, becuase I am older than her. I remember her first word was *“daow”*, an approximation of the cat’s meow, and not the Chinese metaphysical concept for the natural way of bein (Tao / Dao). She soon learned the word “cheese” and began referring to anything edible as *“cheese”*. When hungry, she would demand cheese: *“where’s cheese!?”*. As her vocabulary grew so did her specificity for naming food products, she started calling all meat *“chicken”*, except for chicken which she called *“meat”*. Typical category mistakes for a young language learner.

The best example was when she was 4 year old, and she told daddy that while he was at work mummy had taken her to Jim’s place and locked her in a cupboard with Jim’s kids. To which Dad requested clarification from Mum who at the time was pissing herself laughing. Because Mum had actually gone to the gym, and my little sister spent the day in the children's play area, which was behind a lockable door… a likely story.

## Intentional creative
- Using a pre-existing word to describe an alien concept.
	- [Metaphorisation](public/Metaphorisation.md)

Whether unintentionally calling your teacher Mum or intentionally calling your teacher a cunt, it is a deviation from the conventions which govern speech (more broadly it’s a deviation from social norms, but this is a note on verbal jokes not practical jokes). Calling your teacher Mum or cunt is surprising, and potentially amusing to the right audience.

Even the jokes of professional comedians can be explained as intentional deviation from speech conventions.

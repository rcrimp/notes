title: Circle of expectations

> The Circle of Expectations is Keith Johnstone’s name for the cultural assumptions and associations that cumulatively define the improvisor’s dramatic world

Keith Johnston
1. Be more obvious
2. Stop being original

Suppose a player is on a beach.
Related concepts in the audience’s mind: sea, palm trees, sand, seagulls, etc. 
These *related concepts* are the circle of expectations.
The audience would not be surprised if any of these appear in the story to be developed.

The player finds a treasure chest buried in the sand.
This is within the circle of expectations.

The player finds no treasure, just a lone crab.  
This is within the circle.

The crab starts talking to our character, telling her it knows where the treasure is really hidden.
This is a further towards the edge of the circle.  
But since we are previously established pirate's buried treasure
Perhaps borderline, within the circle of expectations.

It starts raining meatballs and tomato sauce on the beach
This is so WAY outside of the circle of expectations
The audience will disconnect.
The player is trying too hard to be original or funny.

## Tilts
The talking crab is a tilt
it breaks the established pattern

## Be More Boring
By trying to be boring, you’ll usually become more interesting because you’ll be more present and spontaneous.
